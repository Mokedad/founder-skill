# Board: Mama Fit

**Vote: 2 fund if, 1 pass. Average score 3.7 out of 10.** (Offers 4 fund if, Product 4 fund if, Monopoly 3 pass.)

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **Safety and credibility (all three).** Diastasis and pelvic floor work is clinical. No qualified designer or reviewer is named, and there is no red-flag path. One injured user could end the brand, and without credibility buyers won't believe it works.
2. **45 minutes a day fights the customer (Offers, Product).** The target is mums with no time.
3. **Free 5 weeks against paid 8 weeks (Offers, Product).** The free program gives away most of the result. Being cheap is the only reason to pay.
4. **Commodity with no moat (Offers, Monopoly).** Free YouTube postpartum workouts and big apps already exist. Nothing is ten times better yet.
5. **Distribution is unproven (Monopoly).** Instagram reach and cost per buyer are unknown. A one-off AUD 19.99 sale needs very cheap customers.

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] A women's health physio designs or reviews the program, is named on it, and there is a screening plus a "stop and see someone" path (all three members)
- [ ] The default daily session is about 15 to 20 minutes, with longer sessions optional (Offers, Product)
- [ ] The free program is a short quick win, and the paid program is a clear step up with check-ins, a diastasis self-check, community and a guarantee (Offers, Product)
- [ ] Pre-sale clears 3+ paid pre-orders by 20 November 2026 from people who are not friends, with cost per buyer recorded (all three)
- [ ] Interviews show how much time mums really have (Offers, Product)
- [ ] Videos are hosted in the product, not handed off to YouTube, and a human backs up the bot (Product)
- [ ] A narrow first market a copycat can't easily follow, for example diastasis recovery for time-poor Australian mums (Monopoly)

Open questions for other skills: competitor prices and complaints (`/founder-competitors`), would buyers pay (`/founder-consumer`), whether AUD 19.99 one-off can ever be profitable against acquisition cost (`/founder-cfo`).

## Where they disagree

Offers and Product think the pain is real and the offer can be fixed with credibility, shorter sessions and a better paid step. Monopoly says even a fixed offer is easy to copy, and passes until there is a defensible niche and proof of cheap distribution.

## The strongest version the board can see

This is not quite what was pitched. **A physio-reviewed, 15-to-20-minute-a-day diastasis and pelvic floor recovery program for time-poor Australian mums, sold by a mum of three who has been through it.** It would offer a free 1-week quick start, a paid 8-week program with weekly check-ins, a diastasis self-check, a small community, and videos in the app. The physio partner and the founder's story would be the moat. The price would be set by testing, not fixed at AUD 19.99.
