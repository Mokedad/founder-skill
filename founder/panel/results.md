## Why they pass

| reason | buyers | in their words |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| habit | 12 | "I already pay every month for a big fitness app that has a postpartum plan, and I'm using it in my baby's naps. I'd probably grab the free week to try it, but I can't see why I'd pay for a second program that does the same job." (P001) · "I already go to my mums' group and a mums-and-bubs class, and that's where I actually turn up because people expect me there. I'd grab the free week, probably do two sessions on my own at home, and then it'd slide with two kids running around." (P003) |
| trust | 3 | "I'd happily do the free week, but for my pelvic floor I'd rather spend the money on an actual women's health physio appointment and do the exercises she gives me. (P004) · "It's made by a mum of three, not a clinician, and 'reviewed by a women's health physio' doesn't tell me who that physio is. I'd probably poke at the free week, but I wouldn't pay or follow a diastasis program until my own physio has looked at it." (P012) |
| need | 1 | "I'd grab the free week during a night feed, but between that and all the free postpartum core stuff from the creators I already follow, I honestly don't think I'd need to pay for the full 8 weeks. I also haven't heard of this founder, and none of my mum-fitness people are recommending her." (P010) |

## What would flip a no

- If the free week showed the physio-reviewed diastasis check and the founder's weekly check-ins actually fixed my tummy gap where my current app's generic plan hasn't, I'd pay for the full 8 weeks.
- If it ran alongside my weekly class, or the founding group met up in person now and then, so I had real people keeping me accountable.
- If my own physio looked at it and told me it's a good match for what she's prescribed, or if the physio who reviewed it was named with her credentials and did some of the check-ins.
- If I actually finished the free week and noticed I was leaking less when I sneeze, I'd pay the $19.99 for the rest.
- If the free week showed my tummy gap actually closing, or my physio said this is better for diastasis than my current app's plan, I'd cancel my subscription and pay the $19.99 instead.
- A live element I have to show up for, like a scheduled group session on Zoom or a monthly in-person meet-up in my area, so the accountability feels real and not just messages in a chat.
- One of the mum-fitness creators I follow posting about doing it herself, or my own physio recommending it, especially if it clearly fixed the tummy gap better than my current app.
- If the free week showed me it was clearly better for my tummy gap and pelvic floor than my app, like a physio actually checking my progress or the check-ins really keeping me going, I'd cancel the app and switch to this.
- If a mum-fitness creator I follow recommended it, or if the free week actually fixed my tummy gap and I felt properly stronger, I'd pay the $20 to keep going.
- If I actually got through all 4 free sessions in the quick-start week during naps and the diastasis check showed I have a gap that the YouTube stuff isn't dealing with, I'd pay the $19.99.
- The physio named with her credentials and clinic, ideally having designed the diastasis and pelvic floor parts herself, or my own physio saying the program is fine for me.
- A named, AHPRA-registered women's health physio who co-designed the program and says clearly who it suits, plus my own physio saying it's fine for my gap.

Buyers say they would buy **1.0 times** in the first month on average.
