# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- A real, specific moment of need: a mum 6 weeks to 18 months postpartum who wants her strength back and has little time.
- The core can be stated simply: a home strength program built for postpartum recovery.
- Low price (AUD 19.99, one-off) and a free starter program make the first step easy.
- The founder has lived the problem, which can shape the details a generic program misses.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Unsafe or unowned craft in the parts nobody sees.** Pelvic floor and diastasis recovery are clinical. Who designed the program and how it handles red flags is unknown. One injured user ends the brand.
2. **The experience fights the customer.** "Mums with no time" meets 45 minutes a day, 8 workouts and 3x10 sets. The first five minutes may feel like homework, not relief.
3. **Seams everywhere.** Instagram, then a web app, then YouTube links, then a bot guide, then an in-app purchase. Each hand-off is a place to drop out, and an automated guide with no human may not catch a user who is struggling or in pain.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one thing Mama Fit does better than anything else?
- Walk me through a tired mum's first five minutes: where is she, how long does she have, what does she tap?
- Who designed the exercises, and what does the app do when a user reports pain, leaking or doming?
- Why 45 minutes? What would a 10-15 minute version cut, and what would it lose?
- Why is the free program 5 weeks and the paid one 8? What does the buyer get in weeks 6-8 that she could not get free?
- What do you control if YouTube shows an ad or a competitor's video next to yours?
- What happens when the bot cannot answer?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A qualified professional (for example a women's health physio) designs or signs off the program, with a clear screening and "stop and see someone" path.
2. Cut to the one thing: a short daily session that fits a nap time, tested on real mums before anything else is built.
3. Own the experience end to end: videos hosted in the product, no YouTube hand-off, and a defined human fallback for the automated guide.
4. Make free versus paid a clear, honest step up, not just extra weeks.
5. The planned interviews and pre-sale go ahead and clear the stated bar (3+ paid pre-orders by 20 November 2026), with interviewees who are not friends.
