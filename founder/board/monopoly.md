# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- A small, defined starting market: Australian mums 6 weeks to 18 months postpartum who want home strength work.
- The founder's lived experience (mum of three, friends already asking) is the seed of a brand, the one moat a small digital business can realistically build.
- Distribution is named (her Instagram), and a cheap pre-sale test has a clear bar: 3+ paid pre-orders by 20 November 2026.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No secret, no moat.** YouTube-linked workouts, 3 sets of 10, no proprietary method or network effect. A copycat could rebuild it quickly. Nothing stated is ten times better than free postpartum videos. Who else sells to this buyer is an open question for the competitor scout.
2. **Distribution is unproven.** Follower count, reach and acquisition cost are unknown; 10 sign-ups, some possibly friends, none spoken to. At a one-off AUD 19.99 with no repeat revenue, each customer must be very cheap to acquire, and there is no evidence yet that they are.
3. **Credibility in a clinical niche.** Diastasis and pelvic floor work could set it apart, but who designed it and how safety is handled is open. Without qualified input, the one differentiator is also the biggest liability.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about postpartum recovery that other programs get wrong?
- Who else sells a postpartum home program to your followers, and why pick yours?
- How many followers do you have, and how many are mums in the target window?
- Who designed the diastasis and pelvic floor work, and what qualifies them?
- What stops a bigger fitness creator copying this in month six?
- Why 45 minutes a day for mums with no time, and is AUD 19.99 one-off the business or just the entry point?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). What would move it to FUND IF:
- The pre-sale clears its bar with strangers, not friends,, with cost per buyer recorded.
- A qualified physio or postpartum specialist co-designs and names themselves on the program, making the clinical niche a credible brand position.
- A narrower first market she can plausibly dominate (for example, diastasis recovery for time-poor Australian mums), with a reason a copycat cannot follow.
